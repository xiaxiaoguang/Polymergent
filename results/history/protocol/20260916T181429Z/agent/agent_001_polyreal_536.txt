gold: 1. **Defect One: Storing hazardous chemicals in a vacuum drying cabinet (non-explosion-proof).**
    * **Analysis:** This is a seriously non-compliant storage method. Vacuum drying cabinets are not designed for chemical storage and lack explosion-proof features. Initiators commonly used in polymer experiments (such as BPO) and many monomers/solvents are flammable, explosive, or unstable.
    * **Regulatory Requirement:** Flammable, explosive, highly toxic, and other hazardous chemicals must be stored in dedicated chemical safety cabinets or hazardous material warehouses that meet fire and explosion-proof standards.
    * **Severe Consequences:** If electrical components (such as heating wires, relays) inside the vacuum cabinet produce sparks or malfunction, they could ignite the accumulated flammable vapors or cause the decomposition and explosion of unstable initiators, leading to catastrophic outcomes.

2. **Defect Two: Lack of oxygen concentration alarm devices in areas using compressed gases (especially inert and oxidizing gases).**
    * **Analysis:** Using argon (an inert gas) and oxygen (a strong oxidizing gas) in a closed or semi-closed laboratory poses dual risks.
    * **Regulatory Requirement:** (1) If argon or other inert gases leak, they can quickly displace air, reducing indoor oxygen concentration and causing asphyxiation. (2) Oxygen leakage can lead to oxygen enrichment, significantly increasing fire hazards and making ordinary materials highly flammable.
    * **Severe Consequences:** Oxygen concentration detectors must be installed (usually recommended at a height of about 1.5 meters from the ground to cover the breathing zone) to monitor and alarm for both high (>23.5%) and low (<19.5%) oxygen concentrations in real-time. Failure to do so can easily lead to unnoticed asphyxiation deaths or uncontrollable fires.

3. **Defect Three: The laboratory is not equipped with emergency shower and eyewash facilities as per regulations.**
    * **Analysis:** Polymer experiments extensively use chemicals with strong corrosiveness (such as acid-base initiators), high permeability (such as DMF, DMSO), and toxicity.
    * **Regulatory Requirement:** Laboratories handling hazardous chemicals must have emergency shower and eyewash facilities installed within a 10-second (approximately 15 meters) walking distance from potential danger sources, ensuring smooth water flow and qualified water quality.
    * **Severe Consequences:** In the event of a large-scale chemical splash (especially strong acids or bases) on the body or eyes, if not flushed thoroughly and continuously within the initial 'golden 10-15 seconds,' it can cause irreversible severe chemical burns, blindness, or percutaneous poisoning. Ordinary sinks cannot replace professional eyewash and shower equipment.

Placing these initiators in a heated vacuum oven can raise them above their SADT, triggering autocatalytic/explosive decomposition — an organic peroxide/azo explosion and fire hazard. This is unacceptable because these classes of initiators must never be dried/stored with active heating.

2. **Incompatible chemical co-storage**: Initiators (peroxides/azo compounds, classified as oxidizing/explosive-prone substances) are stored together with air-sensitive reagents (pyrophoric or water/air-reactive chemicals) in the same confined cabinet. This violates chemical segregation rules — any leakage or container breakage could bring an oxidizer/peroxide into contact with a reactive/pyrophoric substance, causing fire or explosion. Incompatible hazard classes must be physically separated.

3. **Unsafe proximity and lack of blast/venting protection**: The vacuum drying cabinet containing explosive-prone peroxides is placed directly next to the fume hood without adequate separation distance, explosion-proof construction, or pressure-relief/venting for decomposition gases. A vacuum chamber also risks implosion of glassware. This configuration exposes personnel and the active fume hood workspace to blast/fire propagation in the event of initiator decomposition, which is unacceptable for a facility handling explosive-classified chemicals.
