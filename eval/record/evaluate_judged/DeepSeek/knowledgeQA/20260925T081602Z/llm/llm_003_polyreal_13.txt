gold: Based on the specific requirements of the three products, the recommended polymerization methods are as follows:

1. **Product A (millimeter-sized porous particles)**: **Suspension polymerization** should be selected. The basis is that suspension polymerization is a method of polymerizing oil-soluble monomer droplets dispersed in water, resulting in millimeter-sized spherical particles, which perfectly match the size range required for Product A. Additionally, by adding a 'porogen' (a substance that is a good solvent for the monomer but a poor solvent for the polymer) to the polymerization system, phase separation can occur during polymerization, ultimately yielding resin particles with a permanent porous structure, which is a key structural feature needed for chromatographic separation media.

2. **Product B (micron-sized, highly uniform particle size)**: **Dispersion polymerization** should be selected. The basis is that a significant feature of dispersion polymerization is its ability to produce micron-sized polymer particles with a very narrow particle size distribution (i.e., monodisperse). For applications requiring use as standard references for instruments, the high uniformity of particle size is a crucial technical indicator, and dispersion polymerization is the ideal method to achieve this goal.

3. **Product C (nano-sized particles)**: **Emulsion polymerization** should be selected. The basis is that the reaction site of emulsion polymerization is in nano-sized micelles, resulting in stable polymer latex, where the polymer particle size is usually at the nanoscale (tens to hundreds of nanometers). This size range is very suitable as a base material for latex paints, capable of forming dense, uniform paint films.
