gold: The main challenge encountered in copolymerization reactions is that the copolymerization reactivity ratios (i.e., reaction activities) of functional monomers and conventional monomers often do not match. This is especially true for olefinic functional monomers with bulky functional groups, whose copolymerization reactivity ratios are usually much lower than those of conventional monomers. This mismatch leads to different consumption rates of the two monomers, with the more reactive monomer being consumed preferentially. As a result, the polymer chain segments formed in the early and late stages of polymerization differ significantly in composition, ultimately leading to an uneven distribution of functional groups throughout the polymer chain. This is mainly caused by the 'steric hindrance effect,' where the larger functional groups hinder the monomer from approaching the active center of chain growth, thereby reducing its reactivity. To improve the uniform distribution of functional groups, process techniques such as controlling the monomer concentration can be employed. For example, instead of using batch polymerization with a one-time feed, a 'semi-continuous' or 'continuous addition' method can be used, where the more reactive monomer (conventional monomer) is slowly added to the polymerization system. This maintains a relatively constant instantaneous concentration ratio of the two monomers throughout the reaction, resulting in a copolymer with a more uniform chemical composition.
parsed: The challenge is compositional drift / non-uniform incorporation of the functional monomer. Because the functional monomer and conventional monomer usually have different copolymerization reactivity ratios (r1 ≠ r2), they are consumed at different rates. A bulky functional group often causes steric hindrance, making the functional monomer less reactive and more poorly incorporated, so the comonomer feed composition changes with conversion and the resulting copolymer has a non-uniform distribution of functional groups along the chain and among chains.

This can be improved by:
- Semi-batch / starved-feed monomer addition: continuously feeding the more reactive monomer to keep the comonomer ratio constant.
- Controlled/living radical polymerization (ATRP, RAFT, NMP): gives more uniform chain growth and composition.
- Using protected or less bulky functional monomers to better match reactivity ratios, followed by deprotection.
- Post-polymerization modification of a well-defined precursor copolymer.
