**Reasoning:**

Polymer-supported reagents work by covalently anchoring the reactive small molecule to an insoluble (or soluble-but-precipitable) polymer backbone/resin bead. This changes the physical form from a volatile liquid to a solid, and confines the reactive/toxic functionality within a macromolecular matrix, while chemistry at the reactive site is largely retained.

**Operational safety:**
- Converts a liquid, volatile, toxic reagent into a solid, non-volatile resin — eliminates inhalation hazard, skin/eye contact risk from splashing, and vapor release during weighing, transfer, and charging.
- Lowers vapor pressure to near zero, reducing flammability/explosion risk and the need for specialized fume-hood/closed-transfer engineering controls.
- Improves chemical stability: the polymer matrix sterically/environmentally shields the reactive group from moisture, air, or heat, preventing premature decomposition, self-reaction, or hydrolysis that plagues the free small molecule — so it can be stored, shipped, and dispensed like a normal solid reagent (weighed on a bench, not under inert-gas/cryo conditions).
- Reduces personal protective equipment burden and risk of accidental exposure during large-scale (pilot/plant) handling; a spilled solid resin is far easier and safer to contain/clean than a volatile toxic liquid.

**Reaction process:**
- Simplifies workup: after the acylation is complete, the spent reagent (now bearing the leaving group/byproduct) remains attached to the insoluble polymer and is removed by simple filtration, rather than aqueous extraction, distillation, or chromatography needed to strip a small-molecule byproduct from solution.
- Enables reagent excess to drive reactions to completion without complicating purification — excess polymer-bound reagent is simply filtered off along with used reagent, so purification of the product is not burdened by residual small-molecule acylating agent or its byproducts.
- Gives cleaner, higher-purity product with less contamination from over-acylation, side reactions, or reagent-derived impurities (important for pharmaceutical GMP purity requirements).
- Permits the polymer support to potentially be regenerated/reloaded and reused (recyclability), reducing raw material consumption and waste (E-factor), consistent with green chemistry principles (waste prevention, safer auxiliaries, catalysis/reagent economy).
- Facilitates continuous-flow or column-based processing (packed-bed reactor), improving process control, reproducibility, and scalability, and enabling straightforward reagent regeneration in place.
