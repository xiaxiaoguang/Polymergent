1. **Uniform micro/nano-porous structure (controlled pore size, typically 0.03–1 µm, with narrow pore-size distribution and high, uniform porosity ~40‑60%)**
   - Provides continuous, evenly distributed ion-transport channels for Li⁺, giving low ionic resistance and uniform current distribution, which improves rate capability and cycling uniformity while preventing local current concentration that could cause dendrite growth or hot spots.

2. **Good electrolyte wettability and high electrolyte uptake/retention**
   - Ensures rapid, complete infiltration of the liquid electrolyte and stable retention during cycling, which lowers interfacial resistance, keeps ionic conductivity high, and prevents "dry" spots that lead to local high current density and lithium plating.

3. **High mechanical strength and dimensional stability (high tensile/puncture strength, low elongation, good tear resistance)**
   - Must withstand the tension during winding/stacking assembly and resist puncture from electrode burrs or growing lithium dendrites; a mechanically robust separator prevents internal short circuits, a primary cause of thermal runaway.

4. **Thermal stability and low thermal shrinkage at elevated temperature (high melting/softening point, minimal shrinkage up to ~150 °C or higher)**
   - Prevents separator shrinkage or melting under abuse conditions (overcharge, external heating, internal short), which would otherwise expose the electrodes directly to each other and trigger thermal runaway; ceramic-coated or high-Tm polymer separators (e.g., PP/PAA-coated, PET, PI) improve this.

5. **Shutdown function with an appropriate shutdown temperature window (e.g., PE melts ~130 °C to close pores) combined with a higher melt-integrity temperature (PP layer, ~165 °C) in multilayer (PP/PE/PP) designs**
   - Automatically closes the pores when the battery overheats, cutting off ion transport and stopping the reaction before a full thermal runaway occurs, while the higher-melting outer layer maintains mechanical integrity to avoid a complete short circuit even after shutdown.

6. **Chemical and electrochemical stability (inertness to electrolyte solvents, salts, and electrode materials over the operating voltage window)**
   - Avoids side reactions, gas generation, or degradation that would reduce cycle life or generate safety hazards.

7. **Low and stable electrical resistance (ionic conductivity high, electronic conductivity essentially zero)**
   - Guarantees efficient Li⁺ transport while completely blocking electron flow between electrodes, which is the fundamental function of a separator, ensuring both high rate performance and preventing internal short-circuit currents.

8. **Uniform thickness and flatness with good flexibility for winding/stacking**
   - Ensures consistent gap between electrodes, uniform pressure distribution, and manufacturability without cracks or pinholes, which is essential for consistent cell performance and safety.

9. **Chemical/functional surface modification (e.g., ceramic coating, polymer coating such as PVDF for adhesion) to enhance wettability, thermal stability, and adhesion to electrodes**
   - Improves safety margin (higher thermal shrinkage resistance) and reduces interfacial resistance, further boosting cycle life and rate performance.

In summary, an ideal separator must combine a well-controlled porous structure for efficient, uniform ion transport, high mechanical and thermal stability to prevent internal short circuits under mechanical or thermal abuse, a shutdown mechanism to halt runaway reactions, and chemical inertness/good wettability for long-term electrochemical stability — all of which together ensure the battery operates safely under normal and abusive conditions while delivering high energy density, rate capability, and cycle life.
