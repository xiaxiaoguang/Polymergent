## Definition & Composition

Ziegler–Natta catalysts polymerize 1-alkenes (alpha-olefins) via the reaction: n CH₂=CHR → −[CH₂−CHR]ₙ−

**Heterogeneous catalysts** (dominant industrially):
- Active ingredient: TiCl₄ or TiCl₃
- Support: MgCl₂ (majority of modern catalysts)
- Carrier: microporous silica spheres (30–40 μm diameter)
- Cocatalyst: organoaluminum compounds, typically Al(C₂H₅)₃
- Modifier: ester of aromatic diacid or diether

**Homogeneous catalysts** (soluble):
- Metallocenes with cocatalyst (typically MAO, −[O−Al(CH₃)]ₙ−)
- Common form: Cp₂MCl₂ (M = Ti, Zr, Hf); Cp rings often linked by bridges

## Stereoregularity Control

Polymer tacticity determined by catalyst chirality and geometry:
- **Isotactic**: all stereogenic centers CHR share same configuration; crystalline
- **Syndiotactic**: alternating configurations; crystalline
- **Atactic**: random arrangement; amorphous

Catalyst design controls tacticity through steric hindrance of bulky ligands and active-site geometry. Example: achiral bis(2-phenylindenyl)zirconium dichloride produces isotactic polymers; chiral variant produces syndiotactic polymers.

## Industrial Applications & Scale

Commercial production since 1956. As of 2010, >100 million tonnes annually of polyolefins produced using Ziegler–Natta and related catalysts (Phillips). Major processes: BASF gas-phase (1960s), Unipol fluidized-bed (1968 for polyethylene; mid-1980s for polypropylene). Discovery of MgCl₂ enhancement (1970s) enabled LLDPE resins and fully amorphous copolymers.
