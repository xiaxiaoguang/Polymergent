## Definition & Purpose
A protecting group is a chemical modification introduced to a functional group to achieve chemoselectivity in multistep organic synthesis. It temporarily masks reactive functional groups, then is removed (deprotection) after the desired reaction. Common in peptide/oligosaccharide/nucleotide synthesis due to repetitive functional groups.

## Orthogonal Protection Strategy
Multiple protecting groups on the same molecule can be selectively removed in any order using different cleavage conditions:
- **Benzyl ester**: removed by hydrogenolysis
- **Fmoc (fluorenylmethylenoxy carbamate)**: removed by bases (e.g., piperidine)
- **tert-Butyl ether**: removed by acids (e.g., trifluoroacetic acid)

Critical for solid-phase synthesis and carbohydrate chemistry where hydroxyl groups have similar reactivities.

## Cleavage Methods
- **Acid-labile & Base-labile**: broad utility
- **Fluoride-labile (silicon groups)**: removed by fluoride ions with high selectivity; steric hindrance determines which silicon groups cleave
- **Enzyme-labile**: lipases cleave ethers at pH 5-9, 30-40°C; high substrate specificity
- **Reduction-labile**: catalytic hydrogenation removes benzyl groups (ethers, esters, urethanes, carbonates)
- **Oxidation-labile**: methoxybenzyl ethers cleave to quinomethide via CAN or DDQ
- **Photolabile**: o-nitrobenzyl and similar chromophores activated by appropriate wavelengths
- **Allyl compounds**: isomerize via noble metals; enol ether/enamine hydrolyzes in weak acid

## Common Alcohol Protecting Groups
**Silyl ethers**: TMS (cleaved by KF, acetic acid); TES (10-100× more stable); TBDMS/TBS (cleaved by TFA, TBAF); TIPS; TBDPS (100-250× slower than TBS)
**Esters**: Acetyl (Ac), Benzoyl (Bz, more stable), Pivaloyl (Piv, most stable)
**Acetals/Hemiacetals**: easily cleaved by weak acids
