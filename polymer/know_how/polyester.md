## Definition
Polyester: polymers with one or two ester linkages per repeat unit in the main chain. Most common type is polyethylene terephthalate (PET), a commodity plastic. ~30.5 million metric tons produced worldwide in 2019.

## Types and Properties

**Aliphatic linear (high MW, Mn >10,000):** Low-melting (40–80 °C), semicrystalline, poor mechanical properties, hydrolytically unstable, biodegradable. Used in packaging, disposable items, agricultural mulch, biomedical applications.

**Aliphatic–aromatic (PET, PBT, PTT):** High-melting (160–280 °C), semicrystalline, excellent mechanical properties. Used in engineering thermoplastics, fibers, films.

**Wholly aromatic:** Superior mechanical properties and heat resistance; high-performance applications.

**Unsaturated polyesters (UPR):** Thermoset resins, cross-linked; used as matrices in composites, fiberglass laminates, auto-body fillers, yacht hulls.

**Liquid crystalline polyesters:** High heat-resistance and mechanical properties; used in abradable seals for jet engines.

**Thermoplastic polyester elastomers (TPEE):** Rubber-like behavior.

## Key Properties
- High tenacity, E-modulus, low water absorption, minimal shrinkage
- Aromatic content increases: glass transition temperature, melting point, thermal stability, chemical stability, solvent resistance
- Tend to shrink from flames and self-extinguish upon ignition
- Less fire-resistant than some alternatives; can melt when ignited
- High water, wind, environmental resistance compared to plant fibers

## Applications
Clothing (blended with natural fibers for wrinkle/tear resistance), composites, coatings, aerospace, packaging.
