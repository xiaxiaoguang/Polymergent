## Definition & Structure
Polyethylene (PE) is the most commonly produced plastic, a polymer with chemical formula (C₂H₄)ₙ made from ethylene monomer. Exists as mixture of similar polymers with various chain lengths n. Types include low-density (LDPE), medium-density (MDPE), high-density (HDPE), linear low-density (LLDPE), and ultra-high-molecular-weight (UHMWPE). Properties modified by crosslinking or copolymerization.

## Physical & Chemical Properties
- **Melting point:** LDPE 105–115°C; MDPE/HDPE 120–130°C; theoretical upper limit 144–146°C
- **Combustion:** occurs above 349°C; burns slowly with blue flame (yellow tip); continues burning when flame removed; produces drip
- **Chemical resistance:** excellent; not attacked by strong acids/bases; resistant to gentle oxidants/reducing agents
- **Solubility:** insoluble at room temperature; dissolves at elevated temps in aromatic hydrocarbons (toluene, xylene) or chlorinated solvents
- **Water:** absorbs almost none; low permeability for water vapor and polar gases
- **Gas permeability:** high for non-polar gases (O₂, CO₂, flavorings)
- **Aging:** yellows over time
- **Optical:** transparency varies LDPE > LLDPE > HDPE; reduced by crystallites larger than visible light wavelength

## Electrical Properties
Good electrical insulator; dielectric constant 2.2–2.4 (density-dependent); very low loss tangent. Good electrical treeing resistance but easily electrostatically charged (reducible with graphite, carbon black, or antistatic agents). Commonly used for high-frequency coaxial and twisted pair cable insulation.

## Synthesis
Industrial production requires catalysts for polymerization at mild conditions: Phillips catalyst (chromium trioxide, 1951); Ziegler catalyst (titanium halides + organoaluminium, 1953); metallocene catalysts (1976). Ethylene monomer sourced from petrochemicals or ethanol dehydration; purity <5 ppm for water, oxygen, other alkenes.
