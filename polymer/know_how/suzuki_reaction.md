## Definition & Scope
Suzuki coupling (Suzuki–Miyaura reaction) is a palladium-catalyzed cross-coupling that forms C–C bonds between a boronic acid (R₂–BY₂) and an organohalide (R₁–X) using a base. Widely used for polyolefins, styrenes, substituted biphenyls, and pharmaceutical intermediates.

## Catalytic Cycle & Mechanism
Four main steps catalyzed by Pd(0):
1. **Oxidative addition**: Pd(0) oxidizes to Pd(II), breaking C–X bond; rate-determining step. Retention of stereochemistry on vinyl halides; inversion on allylic/benzylic halides.
2. **Metathesis with base**: Forms Pd(II)–OR intermediate C.
3. **Transmetalation**: Boron ligands transfer to Pd(II) complex via boron-ate intermediate; base-activated.
4. **Reductive elimination**: Pd(II) eliminates product and regenerates Pd(0); retention of stereochemistry.

Base has three roles: forms Pd complex [ArPd(OR)L₂], creates trialkyl borate, accelerates reductive elimination.

## Catalysts & Ligands
- **Phosphine ligands**: Increase electron density; bulkiness aids reductive elimination.
- **N-heterocyclic carbene ligands**: More electron-rich and bulky; stabilize Pd(0), more stable under reaction conditions.
- Palladium nanomaterial catalysts also viable; catalyst loading as low as 0.001 mol% reported.

## Reactivity Order & Advantages
Halide/pseudohalide reactivity: R–I > R–OTf > R–Br ≫ R–Cl

Advantages: mild conditions, low toxicity vs. organotin/organozinc, water-soluble, cheap boronic acids, easy removal of inorganic byproducts. Boronic esters and organotrifluoroborate salts substitute for boronic acids.

## Applications
Scalable for pharmaceutical/fine chemical synthesis. Reported 80 kg scale reaction with 95% yield.
