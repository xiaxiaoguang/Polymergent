## Definition
Graft polymers are segmented copolymers with a linear backbone of one polymer species and randomly distributed branches (grafted chains) of another structurally different species covalently bonded to it.

## Synthesis Methods

**Grafting to:** Backbone chain with randomly distributed functional groups A couples with reactive end-groups of branch polymers. Uses free-radical polymerization, anionic polymerization, ATRP, and living polymerization. Relies on click chemistry and atom transfer nitroxide radical coupling.

**Grafting from:** Backbone is chemically modified to introduce active initiating sites (via copolymerization, post-polymerization, or pre-existing). Number of grafted chains controlled by number of active sites, though branch lengths vary due to kinetic and steric effects. Uses anionic grafting, cationic grafting, ATRP, free-radical polymerization.

**Grafting through (macromonomer method):** Lower molecular weight monomer copolymerized with acrylate-functionalized macromonomer. Ratio of monomer to macromonomer determines graft number; random branch placement results from concentration changes during reaction. Allows heterogeneous or homogeneous branch distribution based on terminal functional group reactivity.

## Properties & Applications
Graft copolymers exhibit wormlike conformation and compact molecular dimensions with notable chain-end effects. More thermostable than homopolymer counterparts. Applications: impact-resistant materials, thermoplastic elastomers, compatibilizers, emulsifiers, membranes, hydrogels, drug delivery.

## Example: High Impact Polystyrene (HIPS)
Discovered 1961 by Charles F. Fryling. Low-cost plastic combining polystyrene backbone with polybutadiene (rubber) grafted chains. Used for machined prototypes, structural components, housings, covers where impact resistance, machinability, and low cost required.
