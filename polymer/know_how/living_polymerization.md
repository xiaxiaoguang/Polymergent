## Definition

Living polymerization is chain-growth polymerization where chain termination and chain transfer reactions are eliminated, allowing precise control over polymer synthesis. Distinct from "controlled polymerization," where termination is suppressed (not eliminated) through dormant states.

## Key Characteristics

- **Low dispersity index (Đ)**: narrow distribution of polymer chain lengths due to simultaneous, uniform chain growth
- **Predetermined molecular weight**: number average molecular weight (Mn) increases linearly with percent monomer conversion
- **Fast initiation rate**: initiation much faster than propagation, ensuring all chains grow at same rate
- **Control of end-groups**: active chains can be functionalized before quenching/termination

**Number average molecular weight (Mn)**: 
$$v = \frac{[M]_0 - [M]}{[I]_0}$$
where [M]₀ = initial monomer concentration, [M] = remaining monomer, [I]₀ = initiator concentration

## Main Techniques

- Living anionic polymerization (demonstrated by Szwarc 1956 with styrene/sodium naphthalene in THF)
- Living cationic polymerization
- Living ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP)
- Living free radical polymerization
- Living chain-growth polycondensations

## Applications

- **Block copolymer synthesis**: sequential monomer addition in stages
- **α-olefin polymerization** (polyethylene, polypropylene): Ziegler-Natta initiators enable stereoselective polymerization and high molecular weight products

## Advantages

Enables precision macromolecular synthesis; microstructure and molecular weight control yield novel material properties rare in non-living polymerizations.
