## Definition
Click chemistry is an approach to chemical synthesis emphasizing efficiency and simplicity to join two molecules. It relies on highly favorable reactions that tolerate many functional groups, typically forming C-heteroatom bonds (heteroatom = N, O, S) rather than C-C bonds.

## Key Reaction Classes

**Copper(I)-catalyzed azide-alkyne cycloaddition (CuAAC):** The classic click reaction. Azides react with alkynes to form a 5-membered C2N3 triazole ring, producing only the 1,4-isomer. Limitation: copper is cytotoxic at effective doses. Solutions include water-soluble ligands to minimize dosage. Ruthenium-catalyzed variant (RuAAC) selectively produces 1,5-isomers.

**Strain-promoted azide-alkyne cycloaddition (SPAAC):** Metal-free alternative with improved biocompatibility and reduced cytotoxicity. Widely adopted for bioorthogonal labeling in living systems.

**Strain-promoted alkyne-nitrone cycloaddition (SPANC):** Metal-free, fast kinetics (k₂ up to 60 1/Ms, faster than CuAAC or SPAAC). Produces isoxazoline products. Tolerate substitution on carbon and nitrogen atoms of nitrone. Limitation: isoxazoline less stable than triazole; can undergo rearrangements at biological conditions. Applications include live-cell labeling and protein labeling via serine oxidation to aldehyde.

**Inverse electron-demand Diels-Alder (IEDDA):** Strained cyclooctenes and activated alkenes react with tetrazines. High ring strain makes three- and four-membered cycloalkenes ideal substrates.

## Applications
Bioorthogonal labeling in living cells and complex environments; protein labeling; multiplex labeling; molecular construction mimicking biological assembly.
