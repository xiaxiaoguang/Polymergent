## Definition & Mechanism

Step-growth polymerization: multifunctional monomers react via functional groups to form dimers, trimers, oligomers, then long-chain polymers. Requires high extent of reaction to achieve high molecular weight.

IUPAC recommends distinguishing:
- **Polyaddition**: addition reactions, no small molecules evolved
- **Polycondensation**: condensation reactions, small molecules evolved

## Distinction from Chain-Growth

Step-growth polymerization (reaction by functional groups) differs from chain-growth polymerization (reaction by free-radical or ion mechanism). Note: polyurethane is addition-type but step-growth mechanism.

## Key Historical Contributions

- **Carothers equations**: mathematical descriptions of step-growth systems (kinetics, stoichiometry, molecular-weight distribution)
- **Carothers & Flory**: developed theories on behavior of step-growth systems; Carothers invented Nylon

## Branching & Gel Point

Monomers with ≥3 functional groups introduce branching. The **gel point** is the transition from tree-like topology to cross-linked network, marked by abrupt viscosity change.

## Polymer Classes & Properties

- **Polyester**: high Tg, Tm, good mechanical properties to ~175°C; used in fibers, films
- **Polyamide**: high strength, elasticity, abrasion resistance; ropes, textiles, bearings
- **Polyurethane**: elastomers, fibers, coatings, foams with excellent properties
- **Polycarbonate**: transparent, high impact strength, thermal stability; F-22 canopy
- **Polyether, Polysulfide, Polysiloxane, Polytriazole**: specialized applications
