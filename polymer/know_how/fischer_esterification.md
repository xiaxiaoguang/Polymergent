## Definition
Fischer–Speier esterification is acid-catalyzed esterification of carboxylic acids and alcohols via refluxing. The reaction produces an ester and water as byproduct. Phenols can also be esterified to good-to-quantitative yields.

## Mechanism
1. Acid catalyst protonates carbonyl oxygen, increasing electrophilicity
2. Alcohol oxygen attacks the activated carbonyl carbon
3. Proton transfer to second alcohol molecule forms activated complex
4. Protonation of hydroxyl group creates oxonium ion
5. Water loss and deprotonation yields ester

## Reaction Conditions & Catalysts
- **Temperature:** 60–110 °C
- **Duration:** 1–10 hours
- **Catalysts:** Sulfuric acid, p-toluenesulfonic acid, Lewis acids (e.g., scandium(III) triflate)
- **Solvents:** Often solvent-free or non-polar (toluene, hexane)
- **Water removal:** Dean–Stark distillation, drying agents (anhydrous salts, molecular sieves), excess acid catalyst

## Equilibrium Control
The reaction is thermodynamically reversible and unfavorable without intervention. Shift equilibrium toward products by:
- Large excess of one reagent
- Water removal via Dean–Stark distillation or drying agents
- Ester distillation if lower boiling point than water/reagents

## Advantages vs. Disadvantages
**Advantages:** Simplicity, atom economy vs. anhydrides/chlorides, direct coupling, environmental benefit (avoids corrosive/toxic reagents)

**Disadvantages:** Slow (hours to years), reversible, requires equilibrium manipulation; sensitive to functional groups intolerant of strong acid

## Application
Natural example: wine aging, where acetic acid catalyzes formation of ethyl acetate and other flavor esters.
