## Definition
Dendrimers are highly ordered, branched polymeric molecules with spherical three-dimensional morphology. Synonymous terms: arborols, cascade molecules. Related but distinct: dendrons (typically used interchangeably), hyperbranched polymers (randomly structured), dendronized polymers, and polymer brushes.

## Structure & Properties
Dendrimers are monodisperse and highly symmetric. Monomers organized in generations: core is G0, attached monomers are G1, etc. An n-th generation dendrimer requires n repeated branching cycles. Each successive generation roughly doubles molecular weight and increases exposed surface functional groups.

Three major components: core, inner shell, outer shell—allowing fine control of solubility, thermal stability, and attachment sites.

## Synthesis Methods
**Divergent:** Assembled outward from multifunctional core via reactions (e.g., Michael reaction). Requires full completion at each step to prevent imperfect generations; difficult to purify.

**Convergent:** Built inward from surface molecules toward core. Easier impurity removal and higher monodispersity, but steric crowding limits size.

**Click chemistry:** Alternative routes using Diels-Alder, thiol-ene, thiol-yne, and azide-alkyne reactions.

## Notable Examples
**PAMAM (Poly(amidoamine)):** Most well-known. Core: diamine (typically ethylenediamine). Lower generations (G-0 to G-2) are flexible with no internal space; medium (G-3, G-4) have separated internal regions; very large (G-7+) behave as dense solid particles.

**Arborol (Newkome dendrimer, 1985):** Synthesized via divergent route using nucleophilic substitution, reduction, and activation steps.
