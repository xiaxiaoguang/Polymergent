## Definition
Total synthesis is construction of complex organic compounds, especially natural products, from basic commercially available starting materials using laboratory methods. Semisynthesis refers to partial synthesis or modification of natural compounds.

## Applications & Scope
Natural product synthesis serves multiple fields:
- **Organic chemistry**: Tests and validates new synthetic methods
- **Medicinal chemistry**: Creates bioactive compounds for drug discovery
- **Chemical biology**: Provides research tools for studying biological systems
- **Structure confirmation**: Helps elucidate newly isolated compound structures

Target classes include terpenes, alkaloids, polyketides, polyethers, and natural polypeptides/polynucleotides (e.g., oxytocin, vasopressin—first synthesized 1954).

## Key Methodologies
- **Retrosynthetic analysis**: Planning routes by working backward from target molecule to design effective construction pathway
- **Stereochemical control**: Ensuring correct three-dimensional atomic arrangement for functionality
- **Reaction optimization**: Enhancing yield, selectivity, and efficiency
- **Scale-up considerations**: Adapting lab syntheses for larger production

Modern approaches combine traditional organic methods, biocatalysis, and chemoenzymatic strategies.

## Notable Historical Achievements
- Camphor (Komppa, 1904)—enabled industrial production by 1907
- Quinine (Woodward & Doering, 1944)
- Morphine (Gates, 1952)
- Strychnine (Woodward, 1954)
- Vitamin B12 (Woodward's team, 1972)—over 100 steps
- Paclitaxel/Taxol (Holton, 1994; Nicolaou, 1995)
