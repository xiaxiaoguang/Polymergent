## Definition
The aldol reaction combines two carbonyl compounds (aldehydes or ketones) to form a new β-hydroxy carbonyl compound via nucleophilic addition of an enolized carbonyl to another. Products contain the RCH(OH)CHR'C(O)R" motif. Aldol dimerization involves two identical molecules; cross-aldol (crossed aldol) involves different substrates.

## Mechanism
**Base-catalyzed (moderate base: OH⁻, alkoxide):** Enolate attacks carbonyl, forming alkoxide salt of product, then protonated to aldol.

**Strong base (LDA, NaHMDS):** Irreversible enolate formation; aldol forms only after workup protonation.

**Acid-catalyzed:** Acid-catalyzed tautomerization to enol; acid activates attacking carbonyl via protonation; enol attacks, forming aldol after deprotonation.

## Selectivity & Reactivity Control
Aldols readily dehydrate to unsaturated carbonyl (enone) under harsher conditions (NaOMe/MeOH/reflux). Mild conditions (LDA, THF, −78 °C) suppress dehydration.

For crossed-aldol with unsymmetrical ketones (four possible products): control enolization vs electrophilic form. Simplest: use reactant with only one acidic proton (forced to enolize). Use formaldehyde or benzaldehyde (cannot enolize) to avoid mixtures. If acidity differs substantially, base abstracts most acidic proton.

Kinetic control: form desired enolate quantitatively (ketone + LDA, −78 °C), then slowly add aldehyde—forward aldol faster than retro-aldol and enolate transfer.

## Industrial Applications
Pentaerythritol, trimethylolpropane, 2-ethylhexanol (plasticizer precursor), Lipitor (atorvastatin).
