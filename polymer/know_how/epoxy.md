## Composition & Curing

Epoxy resins (polyepoxides) contain epoxide functional groups. They are cross-linked via curing with hardeners/curatives: polyfunctional amines, acids, anhydrides, phenols, alcohols, or thiols. This produces thermosetting polymers with high mechanical properties, thermal and chemical resistance.

## Key Parameter: Epoxide Equivalent Weight (EEW)

EEW = molecular weight ÷ number of epoxide groups per molecule. Used to calculate stoichiometric hardener mass. Optimal physical properties achieved with stoichiometric or near-stoichiometric hardener ratios.

## Manufacturing Routes

**Bisphenol-based (most common):** Epichlorohydrin (ECH) + bisphenol A → bisphenol A diglycidyl ether (BADGE/DGEBA). Two-stage: ECH added to bisphenol A, then condensation with NaOH releases NaCl and water. Prepolymerization creates higher molecular weight variants.
- Liquid resins: n = 1–2 repeating units
- Solid resins: n = 2–30 units
- "Advancement" process: liquid resin + bisphenol A heated to ~160°C

**Novolaks:** Phenol + formaldehyde → novolak + ECH → epoxyphenol novolak (EPN) or epoxycresol novolak (ECN). 2–6 epoxy groups per molecule. High crosslink density; high temperature/chemical resistance but low mechanical flexibility.

**Aliphatic:** Double bond epoxidation (cycloaliphatic, vegetable oils) or ECH reaction (glycidyl ethers/esters).

## Applications

Metal coatings, fiber-reinforced composites, electronics, electrical insulators, LEDs, adhesives, paintbrush manufacturing.

## Health Hazards

Contact dermatitis, allergic reactions, respiratory issues from vapor and sanding dust (especially uncured compounds).
