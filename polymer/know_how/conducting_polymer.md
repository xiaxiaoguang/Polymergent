## Definition
Intrinsically conducting polymers (ICPs) are organic polymers that conduct electricity with metallic or semiconducting properties. Main advantage: easy to process. Main limitation: weaker mechanical properties than conventional polymers. Electrical properties tunable via organic synthesis and dispersion techniques.

## Molecular Basis of Conductivity
Undoped conjugated polymers: semiconductors/insulators with energy gap > 2 eV; conductivity ~10⁻¹⁰ to 10⁻⁸ S/cm.

**Doping mechanism:** Backbones contain contiguous sp² hybridized carbons with pz orbitals forming delocalized, one-dimensional electronic bands. Oxidative doping (p-type) removes electrons from delocalized orbitals, creating vacancies that allow electron mobility. Even <1% doping increases conductivity several orders of magnitude to ~0.1 S/cm. Reduction doping (n-type) adds electrons to unfilled bands but rarely used in practice.

Analogy: similar to silicon semiconductor doping with phosphorus or boron.

## Synthesis Methods
**Chemical synthesis:** Connect monomers via heating, pressure, light, catalyst. High yield but impure products.

**Electropolymerization:** Apply voltage across reference/counter/working electrodes in monomer solution. Variants: cyclic voltammetry (cyclic voltage) or potentiostatic (constant voltage). High purity but low throughput.

Most polymers prepared via oxidative coupling of monocyclic precursors with dehydrogenation.

## Key Materials & Applications
Common types: polyacetylene, polypyrrole, polyaniline, poly(p-phenylene vinylene) (PPV), poly(3-alkylthiophenes).

Applications: organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs, since late 1980s), solar cells, transistors.
