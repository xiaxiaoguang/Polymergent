## Definition
Polymer degradation is lowering of polymer strength and properties through chemical composition changes. Degradation rates vary widely: biodegradation takes decades while industrial processes can fully decompose polymers in hours.

## Causes and Mechanisms
Major chemical changes: oxidation and chain scission, reducing molecular weight and degree of polymerization.

**Processing degradation (150–320 °C):** Thermal oxidation, thermal degradation, and shear stress during extrusion/molding snap polymer chains. Introduces hydroperoxides as initiation sites for further degradation. Recycled material shows more degradation than virgin.

**In-service degradation:** Heat, light, air, and water are most significant factors. Specific hazards include:
- Chlorine-induced cracking: 5 ppm chlorine (WHO limit) attacks polyethylene, polybutylene, acetal resin; causes hardening and brittleness
- Electronics: prolonged thermal oxidation in transformers/cables; electrical treeing from high-voltage stress on dielectrics
- Chemical/thermal attack in harsh environments

**Thermo-mechanical:** Low-temperature melt is viscous, prone to shear degradation; high-temperature reduces viscosity but increases thermal degradation. Friction causes localized heating.

## Susceptibility
Thermoplastics more susceptible than thermosets (lower cross-linking). Addition polymers (PP, PE, PVC, PS, PMMA) with all-carbon backbones resist degradation. Condensation polymers (PET, PC) with carbonyl groups are susceptible to hydrolysis and UV attack.

## Mitigation
Stabilizers extend lifespan and enable recycling. Lubricants (paraffin wax, zinc stearate) reduce mechanical degradation. Antioxidants inhibit thermal-oxidation.
