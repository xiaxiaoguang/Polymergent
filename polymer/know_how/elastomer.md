## Definition & Structure

Elastomer: a polymer with both viscosity and elasticity, weak intermolecular forces, low Young's modulus, and high failure strain. Composed of monomers containing carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, and/or silicon.

Molecular structure resembles "spaghetti and meatballs"—long polymer chains cross-linked during vulcanization (curing), creating a network. Elastomers can be thermosets or thermoplastic.

## Elasticity Mechanism

Unlike metals (energy elasticity from bond distance changes), elastomer elasticity is **entropy-driven**:
- Unstressed state: polymer chains in random coiled configurations (maximum entropy)
- Stretched state: chains align into ordered configurations (lower entropy)
- Release: thermal motion restores random coiled state

## Material Examples

Natural: cis-1,4-polyisoprene (natural rubber), gutta-percha
Synthetic: isoprene rubber (IR), butadiene rubber (BR), neoprene (CR), butyl rubber (IIR), styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR), EPDM, silicone rubber, fluoroelastomers (Viton), perfluoroelastomers, polysulfide rubber, elastin/resilin proteins

## Shear Deformation (Neo-Hookean Model)

Free energy change per unit volume under deformation:
$$\Delta f_d = \frac{K_B T \nu_{el} \beta (\lambda_{1p}^2 + \lambda_{2p}^2 + \lambda_{3p}^2 - 3)}{2}$$

For pure shear (λ = 1 in one direction), shear stress relates linearly to shear strain:
$$\sigma_{12} = G\gamma$$

where G is shear modulus and γ is shear strain. Low shear modulus correlates to low deformation energy density.
