## Definition
Atom transfer radical polymerization (ATRP) is a reversible-deactivation radical polymerization that forms carbon-carbon bonds using a transition metal catalyst. It enables synthesis of polymers with controlled molecular weight and narrow molecular weight distribution (low polydispersity).

## Mechanism
The dormant species (R-Pn-X) is activated by a transition metal complex via one-electron transfer, generating radicals and oxidizing the metal to a higher oxidation state. This process rapidly establishes an equilibrium heavily shifted toward dormant species with very low radical concentration. Each chain has equal probability of propagation.

**Key equilibrium:** K_ATRP = k_a / k_d (activation/deactivation rate ratio). Too small K inhibits polymerization; too large increases polydispersity.

## Components & Optimization

**Monomer:** Must stabilize propagating radicals (styrenes, (meth)acrylates, (meth)acrylamides, acrylonitrile). Propagation rate is monomer-specific.

**Initiator:** Alkyl halides (preferably bromides) determine number of polymer chains. Fast initiation rate (faster than propagation) ensures low polydispersity. Multi-halide initiators produce star polymers; α-functionalized initiators enable hetero-telechelic polymers.

**Catalyst:** Most critical component. Requirements: two accessible oxidation states (one-electron difference), reasonable halogen affinity, expandable coordination sphere upon oxidation, minimal side reactions. Cu, Fe, Ru, Ni, Os complexes used.

**Ligand:** Solubilizes metal catalyst and adjusts redox potential, controlling activation/deactivation kinetics. Amine-based ligands common with copper. Higher activity ligands permit lower catalyst concentration but risk loss of control.

**Solvent:** Toluene, dioxane, xylene, anisole, DMF, DMSO, water, methanol, acetonitrile, or bulk polymerization.
