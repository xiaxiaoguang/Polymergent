## Definition
The Claisen condensation is a carbon–carbon bond-forming reaction between two esters or an ester and another carbonyl compound in the presence of a strong base, producing a β-keto ester or β-diketone.

## Requirements
- At least one reagent must be enolizable (possess an α-proton capable of deprotonation to form an enolate anion)
- Base must not undergo nucleophilic substitution or addition with carbonyl carbons
- The alkoxy portion of the ester must be a relatively good leaving group (methyl and ethyl esters are commonly used)
- Stoichiometric amount of base required; reaction will not proceed with substrates having only one α-hydrogen

## Base Selection
- **Classic/Dieckmann condensations**: Use sodium alkoxide base (e.g., sodium ethoxide) corresponding to the alcohol formed; alkoxide is regenerated and does not interfere
- **Mixed (crossed) Claisen**: Non-nucleophilic bases like lithium diisopropylamide (LDA) may be used since only one compound is enolizable
- LDA typically avoided in classic Claisen due to enolization of the electrophilic ester

## Reaction Types
- **Classic Claisen**: Self-condensation of two molecules of one enolizable ester
- **Mixed/Crossed Claisen**: One enolizable ester/ketone with one nonenolizable ester
- **Dieckmann condensation**: Intramolecular reaction of a molecule with two ester groups forming cyclic β-keto ester (typically 5- or 6-membered ring)
- **Retro-Claisen**: Base-induced cleavage of β-ketoesters (reverse reaction)

## Mechanism
1. Strong base removes α-proton, forming resonance-stabilized enolate anion
2. Enolate nucleophilically attacks carbonyl carbon of ester
3. Alkoxy group eliminated, regenerating alkoxide
4. Alkoxide deprotonates doubly α-proton, forming new resonance-stabilized enolate (thermodynamic driving force)
5. Aqueous acid added to neutralize enolate and base; β-keto ester isolated
