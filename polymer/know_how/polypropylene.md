## Definition & Structure
Polypropylene (PP) is a thermoplastic polymer produced via chain-growth polymerization from propylene monomer. It belongs to the polyolefins group and is the second-most widely produced commodity plastic. Three main types exist based on tacticity: isotactic (iPP, methyl groups ordered on one side), syndiotactic (sPP, alternating), and atactic (aPP, random). Commercial PP is typically isotactic with isotactic index 85–95%.

## Key Physical Properties
- **Density:** 0.895–0.93 g/cm³ (lowest among commodity plastics)
- **Young's modulus:** 1300–1800 N/mm²
- **Melting point:** iPP 160–166 °C (commercial) or 171 °C (perfect); sPP ~130 °C at 30% crystallinity
- **Brittleness threshold:** Below 0 °C
- **Crystallinity:** iPP 30–60%; sPP slightly less; aPP amorphous (non-crystalline)

## Thermal & Chemical Properties
Isotactic structure creates higher crystallinity, stiffness, and creep resistance than atactic or polyethylene. At room temperature, PP resists fats and most organic solvents except strong oxidants. Elevated temperature allows dissolution in nonpolar solvents (xylene, tetralin, decalin). Melt flow rate (MFR) measures molecular weight and processability; higher MFR reduces impact strength.

## Modifications
Tacticity affects properties: higher isotacticity increases crystallinity, softening point, rigidity, and hardness. Copolymerization with ethylene improves low-temperature impact strength or transparency depending on arrangement.
