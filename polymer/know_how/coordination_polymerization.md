## Definition
Coordination polymerization is catalyzed by transition metal salts and complexes. It produces linear, unbranched polymers with high molar mass and stereoregularity (isotactic or syndiotactic), introducing crystallinity absent in free-radical methods.

## Catalyst Systems

**Heterogeneous Ziegler–Natta catalysts** (1950s)  
- TiCl₄ + trialkylaluminium complexes → Ti(III)-containing solids  
- Catalyze ethylene and propylene polymerization  
- Catalytic center nature uncertain; multiple active site types possible  
- Phillips catalyst produces ~half the world's polyethylene  

**Homogeneous catalysts**  
- Kaminsky catalysts: Group 4 metallocene (Ti, Zr, Hf) + methylaluminoxane (MAO)  
- Post-metallocene catalysts developed in 1990s  
- Well-defined structures; polymerize via Cossee–Arlman mechanism  
- Active sites typically anionic; cationic coordination polymerization also exists  

## Monomer Scope & Limitations

Ziegler–Natta and Kaminsky systems highly active for nonpolar ethylene and propylene but incompatible with polar monomers (vinyl chloride, vinyl ethers, acrylates)—these require free-radical catalysis. Polar monomers poison traditional catalysts.

Ethylene-vinyl acetate copolymers produced by free-radical methods; coordination polymerization of mixed nonpolar/polar monomers remains largely experimental. "Softer" metal catalysts (palladium, nickel) investigated but not commercialized.

## Specialty Applications

- **Polybutadiene**: 2.1 million tons/year (2000); neodymium-based homogeneous catalyst
- **Polysilanes**: Dehydrogenative coupling of silanes via coordination and oxidative addition; not commercialized
- **Polylactide**: Lactides polymerize with Lewis acidic catalysts
