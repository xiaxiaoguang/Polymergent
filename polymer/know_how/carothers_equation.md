## Definition
The Carothers equation relates the number-average degree of polymerization (X̄ₙ) to fractional monomer conversion (p) in step-growth polymerization. It applies to both linear and branched polymer systems.

## Key Equations – Linear Polymers (Equimolar)
**X̄ₙ = 1/(1−p)**

Where:
- p = (N₀ − N)/N₀ = extent of reaction (monomer conversion)
- N₀ = initial number of molecules as monomer
- N = number of molecules after time t

High conversion required: p = 98% yields X̄ₙ = 50; p = 99% yields X̄ₙ = 100.

## Key Equations – Linear Polymers (One Monomer in Excess)
**X̄ₙ = (1+r)/(1+r−2rp)**

Where r = stoichiometric ratio of reactants (excess reactant as denominator, r < 1). At complete conversion: X̄ₙ → (1+r)/(1−r). Example: 1% excess (r = 0.99) limits X̄ₙ to 199 vs. infinity for equimolar case.

## Key Equations – Branched Polymers (Multifunctional Monomers)
**xₙ = 2/(2−pfₐᵥ)**

Where fₐᵥ = average functionality per monomer unit = Σ(Nᵢ·fᵢ)/ΣNᵢ

## Related Equations (Equimolar Case)
- X̄ᵥ = (1+p)/(1−p) (weight-average degree of polymerization)
- M̄ₙ = M₀/(1−p) (number-average molecular weight)
- M̄ᵥ = M₀(1+p)/(1−p) (weight-average molecular weight)
- Đ = M̄ᵥ/M̄ₙ = 1+p (dispersity index; max value 2 at p=1)
