## Definition & Composition
Poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA), also called acrylic or acrylic glass. IUPAC name: poly(methyl 2-methylpropenoate). A transparent, lightweight thermoplastic engineering plastic. Trade names: Plexiglas, Perspex, Lucite, Hesalite, and others.

## Key Physical Properties
- Density: 1.17–1.20 g/cm³ (≈half that of glass: 2.2–2.53 g/cm³)
- Ignition temperature: 460 °C
- Refractive index: 1.4905 at 589.3 nm
- Coefficient of thermal expansion: (5–10)×10⁻⁵ °C⁻¹
- Light transmission: up to 92% of visible light (at 3 mm thickness); ~4% reflection per surface
- UV filtering: blocks wavelengths below ~300 nm
- IR transmission: passes up to 2,800 nm; blocks longer wavelengths
- Water absorption: 0.3–0.4% by weight maximum
- Impact strength: higher than glass and polystyrene, but significantly lower than polycarbonate

## Processing & Joining
- Can be joined via cyanoacrylate cement, heat welding, or chlorinated solvents (dichloromethane, chloroform)
- Laser cutting vaporizes material cleanly but introduces stress-crazing at edges; annealing is required post-processing
- Scratches removable by polishing or surface heating

## Chemical Resistance & Limitations
- Swells/dissolves in many organic solvents; poor resistance to many chemicals
- Superior outdoor environmental stability vs. polystyrene/polyethylene
- Non-modified PMMA is brittle under load and impact; easily scratched
- Modified formulations use comonomers (butyl acrylate for impact, methacrylic acid for heat resistance) and additives
