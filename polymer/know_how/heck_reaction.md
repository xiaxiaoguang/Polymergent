## Definition
The Heck reaction (Mizoroki–Heck reaction) couples an unsaturated halide or triflate with an alkene in the presence of a base and palladium catalyst to form a substituted alkene. It was the first carbon-carbon bond-forming reaction following a Pd(0)/Pd(II) catalytic cycle.

## Catalysts and Conditions
- **Catalysts/precatalysts:** tetrakis(triphenylphosphine)palladium(0), palladium chloride, palladium(II) acetate
- **Ligands:** triphenylphosphine, PHOX, BINAP
- **Bases:** triethylamine, potassium carbonate, sodium acetate
- **Aryl electrophile:** halides (Br, Cl), triflates, benzyl or vinyl halides
- **Alkene requirement:** must contain at least one sp²-C–H bond; electron-withdrawing substituents enhance reaction (acrylates ideal)

## Reaction Mechanism
1. **Step A:** Oxidative addition—palladium inserts into aryl-halide bond
2. **Step B:** Alkene insertion into Pd–C bond (syn addition)
3. **Step C:** Beta-hydride elimination forming new Pd–alkene π complex
4. **Step D:** Reductive elimination regenerates Pd(0) catalyst; potassium carbonate is stoichiometrically consumed

Palladium(II) precursors are typically reduced in situ by triphenylphosphine to Pd(0).

## Stereoselectivity and Applications
- Stereoselective with propensity for **trans coupling** (palladium halide and bulky organic residue rotate away from each other)
- Industrial uses: naproxen synthesis, octyl methoxycinnamate sunscreen production

## Variants
- **Ionic liquid Heck reaction:** proceeds without phosphorus ligand; catalyst immobilized and reusable
- **Heck oxyarylation:** hydroxyl group displaces palladium, forming dihydrofuran ring
- **Amino-Heck reaction:** nitrogen-to-carbon bond formation
