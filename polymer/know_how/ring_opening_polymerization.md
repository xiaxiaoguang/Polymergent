## Definition & Mechanisms

Ring-opening polymerization (ROP) is chain-growth polymerization where a polymer chain terminus attacks cyclic monomers to extend the chain. Reactive centers can be radical, anionic, or cationic.

**Anionic ROP (AROP):** Nucleophilic initiators attack three-member rings (epoxides, aziridines, episulfides). Example: ε-caprolactone initiated by alkoxide. Silicones produced from cyclic siloxanes (e.g., hexamethyltrisiloxane → [Si(CH₃)₂O]₃ₙ).

**Cationic ROP (CROP):** Cationic initiators (Brønsted acids, carbenium ions, onium ions, metal cations) activate monomers (lactones, lactams, amines, ethers). Proceeds via SN1 or SN2 propagation. Can be living polymerization; terminable by nucleophiles. "Backbiting" can form macrocycles; alkyl chain transfer possible.

**Ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP):** Produces unsaturated polymers from cycloalkenes/bicycloalkenes using organometallic catalysts. Mechanism: [2+2] cycloaddition forming metallacyclobutane intermediate. Examples: polynorbornene, polycyclooctene, polycyclopentadiene.

## Monomers

Suitable cyclic monomers: epoxides, cyclic trisiloxanes, lactones, lactides, cyclic anhydrides, cyclic carbonates, amino acid N-carboxyanhydrides, strained cycloalkenes (norbornene), cyclopropane/cyclobutane derivatives.

## Thermodynamics

Enthalpy change ΔHₚ is negative. Polymerizability criterion: ΔGₚ(xy) = ΔHₚ(xy) − TΔSₚ(xy)

At equilibrium ([M]eq):
[M]eq = exp(ΔHₚ°/RT − ΔSₚ°/R)

## Applications

Industrial: nylon-6 from caprolactam. Solvent-free synthesis via resonant acoustic mixing. Radical ROP enables incorporation of functional groups (ethers, esters, amides, carbonates) in backbone.
