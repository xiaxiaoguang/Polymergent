## Definition & Structure

Silicones (polysiloxanes) are polymers with inorganic Si–O backbones but organic polymer properties. General structure: repeating −O−SiR₂−O−SiR₂− units (R = organic group). Silicon–oxygen backbone chain (···−Si−O−Si−O−···) with two organic groups per silicon center. Silicon is tetrahedral; oxygen adopts bent ether geometry. The R₂Si separation makes chains flexible.

## Synthesis

**Polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS)** is the most common silicone:

n Si(CH₃)₂Cl₂ + n H₂O → [Si(CH₃)₂O]ₙ + 2n HCl

Base catalyst required; removed after polymerization. Produces linear chains terminated with Si−Cl or Si−OH groups, or cyclic polymers. Commercial routes use ring-opening polymerization of cyclic siloxanes (e.g., hexamethyltrisiloxane precursor). Consumer applications use silyl acetates instead of chlorides, producing acetic acid rather than HCl.

**Crosslinking:** Cohydrolysis of methyl trichlorosilane and dimethyldichlorosilane creates rubbery or hard textures.

## Properties

- Thermal stability: −100 to 250 °C
- Low thermal conductivity, chemical reactivity, toxicity
- Water-repellent; forms watertight seals
- Does not support microbial growth
- Electrical insulation (formulated conductive or insulative)
- High gas permeability: O₂ permeability ~400× that of butyl rubber
- Cannot achieve gas-tight seals for high pressure or vacuum
- Resistant to oxygen, ozone, UV light

## Applications

Sealants, adhesives, lubricants, medicine, cooking utensils, construction (coatings, fire protection, glazing), automotive (gaskets, trim), medical devices (aeration), food/beverage, pharmaceuticals (FDA-compliant sheeting).
