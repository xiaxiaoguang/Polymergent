## Definition & Curing Process

A thermoset is a polymer hardened irreversibly by curing (heat, radiation, or chemical reaction). Curing creates extensive crosslinking between polymer chains, producing an infusible, insoluble network. The starting material is malleable or liquid; once hardened, it cannot be melted for reshaping (unlike thermoplastics).

## Curing Mechanisms by Resin Type

- **Acrylic, polyester, vinyl ester**: Free-radical copolymerization with unsaturated monomers; initiation via radiation or thermal decomposition of radical initiators
- **Epoxy**: Postcuring induces secondary crosslinking; backbone hydroxyl groups condense to form ether bonds
- **Polyurethane**: Isocyanate + polyol via nucleophilic addition; stoichiometric ratios essential; moisture exposure forms polyureas
- **Phenolic, amino, furan**: Polycondensation with water/heat release
- **Polybenzoxazines**: Ring-opening polymerization with near-zero shrinkage

## Key Properties

Thermosets are stronger than thermoplastics due to 3D crosslinked networks. Higher crosslink density and aromatic content increase heat resistance and chemical resistance but increase brittleness. They decompose rather than melt. New developments permit limited reprocessing via reversible covalent bond exchange.

## Applications

Fiber-reinforced composites (structural parts, aerospace, marine); particulate-reinforced composites (coatings); adhesives; electrical insulators; foams; coatings. Common materials: epoxy, polyimide, polyester, polyurethane, phenolic (Bakelite), melamine, silicone.
