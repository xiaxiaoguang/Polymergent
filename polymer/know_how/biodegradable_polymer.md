## Definition & Classification

Biodegradable polymers decompose via living organisms or chemical processes. Distinct from bioplastics (biologically synthesized, may or may not biodegrade) and compostable plastics (require artificial conditions). Most biodegradable polymers are polyesters; the ester group (RC(O)OR') is susceptible to hydrolysis and enzymatic action.

## Major Types

**Polyhydroxyalkanoates (PHAs):** Naturally produced by microorganisms (e.g., Cupriavidus necator, Alcaligenes latus). Synthesized under nutrient deprivation (phosphorus, nitrogen, oxygen) with excess carbon. Two classes:
- scl-PHA: short-chain (3–5 carbons), e.g., poly-3-hydroxybutyrate (PHB)
- mcl-PHA: medium-chain (6–14 carbons), e.g., from Pseudomonas putida

**Polylactic acid (PLA):** Thermoplastic aliphatic polyester from fermented plant starch (maize, cassava, sugarcane). Compostable but not biodegradable under standard conditions.

**Starch blends:** Starch + plasticizers (thermoplastic). Biodegradability depends on plasticizer type. Biodegradable examples: starch/PLA, starch/PCL, starch/PBAT.

**Cellulose-based:** Cellulose esters (acetate, nitrocellulose) and derivatives.

**Polyesters:** PET susceptible to esterase enzymes; polyglycolic acid (PGA) degrades via hydrolysis to glycolic acid; polycaprolactone (PCL); polybutylene succinate (PBS) degradable by Amycolatopsis sp. and Penicillium sp.

## Applications

Medical sutures (catgut, PGA, Vicryl), packaging films, agricultural mulch films.
