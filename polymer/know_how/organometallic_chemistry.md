## Definition

Organometallic compounds contain at least one direct chemical bond between a carbon atom of an organic molecule and a metal (alkali, alkaline earth, transition, lanthanide, actinide metals, or semimetals like B, Si, As, Se). Bonds to carbon monoxide, cyanide, and carbide are also considered organometallic. Distinction: "metalorganic" compounds contain organic ligands but lack direct M-C bonds.

## Key Structural Concepts

**Metal-carbon bond:** Generally highly covalent. For electropositive elements (Li, Na), the carbon ligand exhibits carbanionic character.

**Hapticity (η):** Describes the number of contiguous ligands coordinated to a metal. Example: ferrocene [(η⁵-C₅H₅)₂Fe] has hapticity 5, with all five carbons bonding equally.

**Kappa (κ):** Denotes ligands binding non-contiguous atoms (e.g., κ²-acetate chelating).

## Stability and Electron Counting

**18-electron rule:** Predicts stability of organometallic complexes, particularly metal carbonyls and hydrides. Uses ionic or neutral (covalent) ligand counting models. Many compounds do not follow this rule.

**d electron count and oxidation state:** Help predict reactivity and preferred geometry.

## Common Examples

Gilman reagents (Li/Cu), Grignard reagents (Mg), organolithium compounds (n-BuLi), organozinc (Et₂Zn), organotin (Bu₃SnH), organoborane (Et₃B), organoaluminium (Me₃Al), tetracarbonyl nickel, ferrocene, methylcobalamin (Vitamin B₁₂).

## Characterization and Handling

**Techniques:** X-ray diffraction, infrared/NMR/UV-visible spectroscopy, electron paramagnetic resonance, elemental analysis.

**Air sensitivity:** Many compounds require air-free handling via glovebox or Schlenk line. Some (e.g., triethylaluminium) are pyrophoric.
