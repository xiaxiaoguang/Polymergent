## Definition & Scope
Wittig reaction (Wittig olefination): converts aldehydes or ketones to alkenes using triphenyl phosphonium ylides (Wittig reagents). Works on sterically hindered ketones like camphor. Tolerates OH, OR, nitroarenes, epoxides, and sometimes esters and amides.

## Mechanism
Ylides react with carbonyl compounds via [2+2] cycloaddition (topology [π2s+π2a]) to form oxaphosphetanes. Under lithium-free conditions, this is concerted and kinetically controlled (no betaine intermediate). With lithium present, intermediates may equilibrate via betaine species (3a, 3b)—termed "stereochemical drift" by Maryanoff and Reitz. Mechanisms differ for aliphatic vs. aromatic aldehydes and ylide types.

## Stereochemistry
**Unstabilized ylides** (R = alkyl): produces (Z)-alkene with moderate-to-high selectivity; adding LiI or NaI in DMF gives nearly exclusive Z-isomer.
**Stabilized ylides** (R = ester/ketone): produces (E)-alkene with high selectivity.
**Semistabilized ylides** (R = aryl): poor E/Z selectivity.

Schlosser modification: converts erythro to threo betaine using phenyllithium at low temperature to afford E-alkene from unstabilized ylides.

## Limitations & Alternatives
Sterically hindered ketones: slow reaction, poor yields (especially with stabilized ylides)—use Horner–Wadsworth–Emmons (HWE) reaction instead. Aldehydes can oxidize, polymerize, or decompose. Tandem oxidation-Wittig forms aldehyde in situ from alcohol. Julia–Kocienski olefination and Still-Gennari HWE modification also available for stereochemical control.
