## Definition
Organic synthesis is the branch of chemical synthesis concerned with constructing organic compounds (molecules of covalently-linked hydrogen, carbon, oxygen, and nitrogen atoms).

## Synthetic Approaches

**Total Synthesis:** Complete chemical synthesis of molecules from simple natural precursors, accomplished via:
- Linear approach: sequential steps producing synthetic intermediates until completion; suitable for simple structures
- Convergent approach: individual preparation of key intermediates, then combined to form desired product

**Stereoselective Synthesis:** Directed reactions producing single enantiomers rather than racemic mixtures. Methods include stereoselective hydrogenations and asymmetric epoxidation. Eliminates need for enantiomeric resolution.

**Retrosynthetic Design:** Planning synthesis backwards from the desired product while adhering to standard chemical rules.

## Key Methodology Considerations
- Maximize product yield with minimum steps
- Ensure adequate purity
- Account for safety, environmental impact, and industrial applicability
- Literature precedent or new route testing

## Separation and Purification Techniques

**Liquid-Liquid Extraction:** Uses density and polarity differences; based on "like-dissolves-like" principle. Non-polar compounds dissolve in non-polar solvents; polar in polar solvents. Immiscible solvents create separable layers.

**Reflux Condensers:** Water-cooled glassware preventing solvent loss during heating. Used in reflux (1 drop/second-few seconds back into flask) and recrystallization (heat until clear, cool to re-precipitate purer product).

**Filtration:**
- Vacuum filtration: suction pulls liquid through Büchner funnel, catches solid product
- Gravity filtration: force of gravity separates product through folded filter paper
