## Definition
A copolymer is a polymer derived from more than one species of monomer. Copolymers from two monomers are called bipolymers. They can be classified by arrangement of constituent units along the chain: linear (single main chain), or branched (with polymeric side chains).

## Copolymer Types
- **Block copolymers**: Two or more homopolymer blocks linked covalently; made via living polymerization (ATRP, RAFT, ROMP, living anionic/cationic). Diblock copolymers have two blocks; triblock have three. Synthesis requires r₁ >> 1 and r₂ >> 1.
- **Alternating copolymers**: Regular A-B-A-B pattern, formed when r₁ and r₂ are close to zero.
- **Statistical/random copolymers**: Monomer sequence follows kinetic probability; common in practice.
- **Periodic copolymers**: Units in repeating sequence (e.g., A-B-A-B-B-A-A-A-A-B-B-B).

## Reactivity Ratios & Mayo–Lewis Equation
Reactivity ratio: r₁ = k₁₁/k₁₂ and r₂ = k₂₂/k₂₁

Mayo–Lewis equation governs relative instantaneous incorporation rates:
d[M₁]/d[M₂] = [M₁](r₁[M₁] + [M₂]) / [M₂]([M₁] + r₂[M₂])

Copolymer composition and structure depend on these ratios.

## Synthesis & Applications
- **Chain-growth polymerization**: ABS, SBR, nitrile rubber, styrene-acrylonitrile, SIS, ethylene-vinyl acetate.
- **Step-growth polymerization**: Nylon-12/6/66, copolyesters.
- Applications: Commercial goods, drug delivery vehicles.

## Characterization
NMR spectroscopy and size-exclusion chromatography determine molecular size, weight, properties, and composition.
