## Definition & Structure

Star-shaped polymers consist of ≥3 linear polymer chains (arms) radiating from a central multifunctional core. The core can be an atom, molecule, or macromolecule. 

**Homostars (regular stars):** arms are chemically identical in length and structure.  
**Heteroarm stars (variegated):** arms differ in chemistry and/or molar mass.

## Nomenclature

IUPAC designates star polymers with "star-" prefix; specify number of arms (f) when known. Example: **6-star-(polyA(f3); polyB(f3))** = 6 total arms, 3 of polyA and 3 of polyB.

## Key Properties

**Rheological characteristics** (vs. linear polymers of same molecular weight):
- Smaller hydrodynamic radii and radii of gyration
- Lower internal viscosity
- Lower melting points, crystallization temperatures, and crystallinity
- Heteroarm stars show higher viscosity and hydrodynamic radii than homostars due to repulsive heterocontacts

**Self-assembly:**
- Higher critical micelle concentrations and lower aggregation numbers than linear analogues
- Heteroarm stars form supramolecular structures (stars, ribbons, core-shell-corona micelles) depending on arm solubility
- Functional groups and solvent choice affect aggregation

## Synthesis Routes

**Arm-first (convergent):** Monofunctional living polymer chains react with multifunctional core; allows pre-characterization and direct control; anionic polymerization common.

**Core-first:** Core acts as initiator.

**Other methods:** sol-gel, group transfer polymerization, ROMP, controlled radical polymerization, living cationic polymerization, ring-opening polymerization.

## Applications

Drug delivery, biomedical applications, thermoplastics, nanoelectronics, emulsion stabilization, inorganic salt solubility enhancement.
