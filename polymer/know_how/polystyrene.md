## Definition and Chemical Structure

Polystyrene (PS) is a synthetic polymer made from styrene monomers. Chemical formula: (C8H8)n. Clear, hard, brittle at room temperature; relatively cheap; poor oxygen barrier. Density: 1.05 g/cm³ (vs. aluminum at 2.70 g/cm³). Properties determined by van der Waals attractions between polymer chains.

## Key Properties

- **Glass transition temperature**: ~100 °C (becomes flowable when heated above this; rigid when cooled)
- **Molar mass**: 100,000–400,000 g/mol (typically few thousand monomers per chain)
- **Tacticity**: Describes alignment of phenyl groups; standard commercial PS is atactic. Isotactic polystyrene exists but not produced commercially
- **Not biodegradable** (per ASTM standards); accumulates as environmental litter, especially in foam form

## Production

Addition polymer formed when styrene monomers polymerize via broken carbon–carbon π bonds forming new σ bonds. Initiators: heat or benzoyl peroxide. Pure styrene polymerizes spontaneously without external initiators. Difficult to depolymerize (new σ bond stronger than original π bond).

## Applications

Protective packaging, packing peanuts, optical disc cases, containers, lids, bottles, trays, cutlery, models, phonograph records. Foamed form (Styrofoam, patented 1944) used for insulation and buoyant applications. Thermoplastic properties enable extrusion, molding, and vacuum forming with fine detail.
