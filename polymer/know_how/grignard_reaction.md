## Definition
Grignard reaction: addition of Grignard reagents (carbon alkyl, allyl, vinyl, or aryl magnesium halides) to carbonyl groups of aldehydes or ketones to form primary or secondary alcohols. Classical vs. modern definitions exist; modern definition extends to reactions with various electrophilic substrates.

## Mechanism & Stereochemistry
- Carbon attached to magnesium acts as nucleophile (more electronegative than Mg), attacks electrophilic carbonyl carbon
- Addition proceeds through six-membered ring transition state
- Stereochemistry predictable via Felkin-Anh model or Cram's Rule for prochiral ketones
- Alternative single electron transfer (SET) mechanism proposed; operative mechanism (polar vs. radical) is substrate-dependent, with carbonyl reduction potential as key parameter

## Conditions & Requirements
- **Anhydrous conditions mandatory**: water causes Grignard reagent to act as base, abstracting labile protons rather than attacking electrophile, preventing product formation
- Inert atmosphere required
- Acidic protons in starting material can be protected by converting to ether or silyl ether prior to reaction

## Variants & Modifications
- **Turbo-Grignards**: modified with lithium chloride; more chemoselective than conventional Grignard reagents; do not react with esters, amides, nitriles
- **Heterometal-modified Grignard reagents**: Copper(I) salts yield organocuprates for 1,4-addition; Cerium trichloride for selective 1,2-addition; Nickel and palladium halides catalyze cross-coupling
