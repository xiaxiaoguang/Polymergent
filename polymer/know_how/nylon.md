## Definition & Structure

Nylon is a family of synthetic polymers characterized by amide linkages connecting aliphatic or semi-aromatic groups. Generally brownish, soft texture, some varieties silk-like. Thermoplastic material.

Two main families:
- **Nylon-XY**: derived from diamines and dicarboxylic acids with carbon chain lengths X and Y. Example: nylon-6,6 with structure (−C(O)(CH2)4C(O)−NH(CH2)6NH−)n
- **Nylon-Z**: derived from aminocarboxylic acids with chain length Z. Example: nylon-6

Properties modified by blending with various additives.

## Applications

- Textiles and fibers: apparel, flooring, rubber reinforcement
- Molded components: automotive and electrical equipment
- Films: primarily food packaging
- WWII: parachutes and parachute cord

## Development History

- **1927**: DuPont initiated polymer research program; Wallace Hume Carothers hired to direct
- **1930**: Team synthesized neoprene and elastic paste (precursor to nylon); Julian W. Hill developed cold drawing method
- **February 28, 1935**: First nylon (nylon-6,6) synthesized by Carothers at DuPont Experimental Station
- **January 29, 1938**: Paul Schlack at IG Farben developed nylon-6 from caprolactam
- **September 1938**: DuPont obtained patent; monopoly achieved
- **December 15, 1939**: First commercial nylon plant opened in Seaford, Delaware
- **1940**: Commercial sale of nylon stockings; 64 million pairs sold first year

First commercial use: nylon-bristled toothbrush (1938). Carothers died before nylon's public announcement.
