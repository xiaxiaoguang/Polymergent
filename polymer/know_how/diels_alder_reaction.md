## Definition & Overview
The Diels–Alder reaction is a [4+2] cycloaddition between a conjugated diene and a dienophile (substituted alkene) forming a six-membered ring (cyclohexene derivative). It reliably constructs two new C–C bonds with good regio- and stereochemical control.

## Mechanism
Concerted pericyclic reaction via single cyclic transition state (no intermediates). Classified as [π4s + π2s] cycloaddition—suprafacial/suprafacial interaction of 4π diene with 2π dienophile system, governed by orbital symmetry.

**Normal electron-demand:** HOMO of electron-rich diene (ψ2) interacts with LUMO of electron-deficient dienophile (π*).

**Inverse electron-demand:** Electron-withdrawing groups on diene lower its ψ3 orbital; electron-donating groups on dienophile raise its π orbital, reversing dominant interaction.

Reaction is thermally initiated (ground state). Stereospecific syn addition; diradical intermediates postulated in some cases.

## Rate Equation
$$\log(k \cdot \text{mol} \cdot \text{s}) = \left(290 \pm 10 - (60.8 \pm 2.8) \text{ Å}^{-1} \cdot R\right) \frac{\text{eV}}{I_D - E_A} - (5.05 \pm 0.39) \frac{\text{mol}}{\text{kJ}} \cdot \Delta H - 28.2$$

where R = distance between diene ends, ID = diene ionization potential, EA = dienophile electron affinity, ΔH = reaction enthalpy. Polar solvents (water, DMF, ethylene glycol) dramatically enhance rates.

## Regioselectivity
Follows ortho-para rule. Maximizing interaction energy by bonding centers with largest frontier orbital coefficients predicts major regioisomer for given diene-dienophile pair.
