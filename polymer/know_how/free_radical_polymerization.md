## Definition
Radical polymerization (RP) is a chain polymerization method where polymers form by successive addition of radicals to monomers. It is highly versatile due to the non-specific nature of radical reactions. In 2001, ~40 billion of 110 billion pounds of U.S. polymers were produced via RP.

## Initiation Methods
An active center is created through two steps: (1) radical generation from initiator, (2) radical transfer to monomer.

**Methods:**
- **Thermal decomposition**: Heat cleaves bonds homolytically in organic peroxides or azo compounds
- **Photolysis**: Radiation (300–400 nm optimal) cleaves bonds; requires high absorptivity, efficient radical generation, solubility, non-yellowing byproducts
- **Redox reactions**: H₂O₂ or alkyl peroxides reduced by Fe²⁺, Cr²⁺, V²⁺, Ti³⁺, Co²⁺, Cu⁺
- **Persulfates**: Dissociation in aqueous phase (useful for emulsion polymerization)
- **Ionizing radiation**: α-, β-, γ-, x-rays eject electrons
- **Electrochemical**: Monomer receives electron at cathode (radical anion) or loses electron at anode (radical cation)
- **Plasma**: Gaseous monomer in electric discharge at low pressure
- **Sonication**: High-intensity ultrasound (>16 kHz) causes cavitation, generating radicals
- **Ternary initiators**: Combination systems (e.g., benzoyl peroxide + metallocene) accelerate polymerization and narrow molecular weight distribution

## Initiator Efficiency
Efficiency factor **f** = fraction of initiator contributing to polymerization. Typical range: 0.3–0.8 (max = 1). Loss mechanisms: primary recombination in solvent cage, recombination outside cage, side reactions producing fewer radicals.

## Propagation
Radical attacks C=C double bond in vinyl monomers, using one electron from π bond. Chain extends until monomer depletion or termination. Duration depends on radical reactivity, chain reactivity, solvent, and temperature.
