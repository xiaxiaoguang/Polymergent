## Definition
Thermoplastic: a polymer that becomes pliable/moldable above its glass transition temperature, solidifies upon cooling, and can be reshaped repeatedly. Differs from thermosets, which form irreversible chemical bonds and decompose rather than melt when heated.

## Key Properties
- High molecular weight; polymer chains associated by intermolecular forces
- Above glass transition temperature and below melting point, physical properties change drastically without phase change
- Amorphous and semi-amorphous types offer high optical clarity but lower chemical resistance and environmental stress cracking resistance
- Brittleness reduced by adding plasticizers, which lower glass transition temperature by increasing chain segment mobility

## Processing & Materials
Common processing: injection molding, compression molding, calendering, extrusion

Major types and applications:
- **PMMA (acrylic)**: glass substitute—aquariums, aircraft windows, lenses, signs, bone cement
- **ABS**: lightweight, high impact resistance—toys, appliances, phones
- **Nylon (polyamide)**: parachutes, clothing, rope, carpets, mechanical parts (gears, screws)
- **PLA**: compostable, from renewable resources (corn, sugar)—3D printing
- **Polycarbonate (PC)**: electronics, automotive/aircraft parts, security glazing; contains BPA; UV-susceptible
- **PEEK**: high abrasion resistance, low flammability, low smoke emission—engineering applications
- **POM (acetal)**: high stiffness, low friction, dimensional stability—precision parts
- **PEI**: high heat distortion, tensile strength—electrical, electronics, automotive parts
- **Polybenzimidazole (PBI)**: exceptional thermal/chemical stability—firefighter gear, spacesuits, fuel cell membranes
- **PES (polysulfone)**: thermal/oxidative/hydrolytic stability, acid/alkali resistance
