## Definition & Properties
Polyvinyl chloride (PVC) is the world's third-most produced synthetic polymer. Annual production: ~40 million tons. White, brittle solid with density 1.4 g/cm³ (higher than polyethylene 0.88–0.96 g/cm³). Composition: ~57% chlorine by mass. Soluble in ketones, chlorinated solvents, dimethylformamide, THF, and DMAc.

## Production Methods
**Suspension polymerization** (80% of production): Produces particles 100–180 μm diameter. VCM and water introduced with initiator and additives; vessel pressurized and continuously mixed. Reaction is exothermic and requires cooling. Volume decreases during reaction (PVC denser than VCM), so water continuously added to maintain suspension.

**Emulsion polymerization** (12%): Produces smaller particles (~0.2 μm).

**Bulk polymerization** (8%).

## Microstructure
Linear polymers with monomers arranged mainly head-to-tail. Atactic stereochemistry (random chloride placement) with a few percent crystallinity from syndiotacticity.

## Thermal Stability
Onset of degradation near 250°C (vs. 400°C for polyethylene). Heat stabilizers minimize HCl loss; metallic soaps common in flexible applications; BaZn stabilizers replace cadmium-based stabilizers.

## Additives & Plasticizers
Unmodified PVC requires additives: heat stabilizers, UV stabilizers, plasticizers, processing aids, impact modifiers, flame retardants, biocides, fillers, smoke suppressors, pigments. 

Phthalate plasticizers dominate; low-molecular-weight (DEHP, DBP) phased out. High-molecular-weight (DINP, DIDP) considered safer. DEHP banned in US children's products (2008); EU requires CMR labeling (2010).
