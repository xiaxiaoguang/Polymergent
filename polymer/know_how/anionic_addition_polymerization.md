## Definition
Anionic addition polymerization is chain-growth polymerization initiated by anions. Living anionic polymerization (demonstrated by Szwarc, 1956) involves no formal termination step—carbanion propagating centers remain active indefinitely absent impurities, enabling precise molecular weight control.

## Monomers
**Vinyl monomers:** CH₂=CHR (styrene, butadiene, isoprene)
**Acrylates:** acrylonitrile, methacrylate, cyanoacrylate, acrolein
**Cyclic:** epoxides, cyclic trisiloxanes, lactones, lactides, cyclic carbonates
Substituents must stabilize negative charge via delocalization. Carbanion centers require protection from base/nucleophile-reactive groups.

## Initiators
- **Highly electrophilic monomers (cyanoacrylates):** weakly nucleophilic initiators (amines, phosphines, halides)
- **Low reactivity (styrene):** strong nucleophiles (butyllithium)
- **Intermediate:** proportional initiator strength

**Initiation mechanisms:**
1. **Electron transfer:** radical anion (e.g., sodium naphthalene) transfers electron to monomer, forming disodium initiator; requires polar solvents
2. **Strong anions:** nucleophilic addition via organometallium compounds, amides, alkoxides

## Living Polymerization Features
Molecular weight: Mn = (Mo[M]₀)/[I], where Mo = repeating unit mass, [M]₀ = initial monomer concentration, [I] = initiator concentration
- All chains initiated simultaneously
- Poisson molecular weight distribution
- Quantitative chain-end functionalization
- Spontaneous termination reduces active anion concentration over time
