## Definition

Molar mass distribution (molecular weight distribution) describes the relationship between number of moles of each polymer species (Ni) and molar mass (Mi). Polymer chains of the same type rarely have identical masses, requiring statistical averaging methods.

## Key Molar Mass Averages

Four averages are standard:

- **Number average:** Mn = (∑ MiNi) / (∑ Ni)
- **Mass average:** Mw = (∑ Mi²Ni) / (∑ MiNi)
- **Z-average:** Mz = (∑ Mi³Ni) / (∑ Mi²Ni)
- **Viscosity average:** Mv = [(∑ Mi^(1+a)Ni) / (∑ MiNi)]^(1/a)

where a is the Mark–Houwink exponent (0.5–0.8, depends on solvent-polymer interactions).

Relationship: Mn < Mv < Mw < Mz

**Dispersity** (polydispersity index) = Mw/Mn; indicates distribution width.

## Measurement Techniques

Each method measures a specific average:
- Osmometry → Mn
- Light scattering → Mw
- Viscometry → Mv
- Analytical ultracentrifugation → Mz
- **Size exclusion chromatography (SEC)/gel permeation chromatography (GPC):** forces polymer solution through cross-linked polymer matrix; high-molecular-mass species elute faster. Commonly paired with differential refractive index detector (concentration-sensitive) or multi-angle laser light scattering (mass-sensitive).

## Factors Affecting Distribution

- Ideal step-growth polymerization: dispersity = 2
- Ideal living polymerization: dispersity = 1
- Polymer fractionation can modify distribution
- Depends on chemical kinetics and work-up procedure
