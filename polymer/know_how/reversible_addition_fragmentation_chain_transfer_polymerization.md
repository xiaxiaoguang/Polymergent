## Definition
Reversible addition−fragmentation chain-transfer (RAFT) polymerization is a controlled radical polymerization technique using thiocarbonylthio compounds (dithioesters, thiocarbamates, xanthates) as chain transfer agents (CTA). Discovered at CSIRO in 1998, it enables synthesis of polymers with low dispersity (Đ), pre-chosen molecular weight, and complex architectures (block copolymers, star, brush, dendrimer, cross-linked networks).

## Key Components & Conditions
Required: radical source (thermochemical initiator or radiation), monomer, RAFT agent, solvent (optional if monomer is liquid). Temperature must balance: appropriate chain growth rate, adequate radical delivery rate, favorable central RAFT equilibrium toward active state. Common initiators: AIBN, ACVA. Compatible monomers: (meth)acrylates, (meth)acrylamides, acrylonitrile, styrene, butadiene, vinyl acetate, N-vinylpyrrolidone.

## Mechanism
**Initiation:** Radical initiator decomposes to fragments (I•) adding to monomer, forming P₁•.  
**Propagation:** Pn• + M → Pn+1•.  
**Pre-equilibrium:** Pn• reacts with RAFT agent forming adduct radical; reversible fragmentation yields R• or polymeric RAFT agent S=C(Z)S-Pn.  
**Re-initiation:** R• reinitiates new polymer chain.  
**Main equilibrium:** Rapid radical interchange shares growth opportunities among active Pn• and dormant species S=C(Z)S-Pn.  
**Termination:** Biradical events or initiator fragment reactions.

## Design Considerations
Z-group: affects S=C bond stability and adduct radical stability, controlling pre- and main-equilibrium rates.  
R-group: must stabilize radical (favor pre-equilibrium right side) but be unstable enough to reinitiate chain growth.  
Product selectivity increases with higher RAFT concentration or lower initiator concentration/decomposition rate.
