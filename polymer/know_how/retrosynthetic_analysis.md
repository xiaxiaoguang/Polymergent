## Definition
Retrosynthetic analysis is a technique for planning organic syntheses by transforming a target molecule into simpler precursor structures, working backwards regardless of actual reactivity. Key terms:
- **Disconnection**: Breaking a bond to form two or more synthons
- **Synthon**: A fragment derived from the target that assists synthesis formation
- **Retron**: Minimal molecular substructure enabling certain transformations
- **Transform**: The reverse of a synthetic reaction
- **Retrosynthetic tree**: Directed acyclic graph of all possible retrosyntheses for a target

## Core Concept
The goal is structural simplification. A synthon and its commercially available synthetic equivalent are identified and reacted to produce the desired product. Databases can be consulted to determine if components already exist in literature, avoiding redundant work.

## Strategic Approaches
- **Functional group strategies**: Manipulation of functional groups reduces molecular complexity
- **Stereochemical strategies**: Transformations like Claisen rearrangement and Mitsunobu reaction establish desired chirality
- **Structure-goal strategies**: Directing synthesis toward desirable intermediates enables bidirectional search
- **Transform-based strategies**: Powerful transforms reduce complexity but are rarely present in complex molecules
- **Topological strategies**: Key bond disconnections identify substructures; prefer disconnections preserving rings; avoid creating rings >7 members

## Tools & Applications
Modern approaches integrate machine learning and AI (e.g., MIT Coley Group, Chemical.AI, Reaxys) with rule-based methods for computational retrosynthesis planning.
