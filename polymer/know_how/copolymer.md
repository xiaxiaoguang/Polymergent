## Definition

A copolymer is a polymer derived from more than one species of monomer. Copolymers from two monomer species are called bipolymers. They are classified by how constituent units arrange along the chain: linear (single main chain), branched (with polymeric side chains), or cross-linked.

## Synthesis Methods

**Chain-growth polymerization:** produces ABS, SBR, nitrile rubber, styrene-acrylonitrile, SIS, ethylene-vinyl acetate.

**Step-growth polymerization:** produces nylon copolymers (nylon-12/6/66) and copolyesters.

**Block copolymer techniques:** ATRP, RAFT, ROMP, living cationic/anionic polymerization, chain shuttling polymerization.

## Composition Control: Reactivity Ratios & Mayo–Lewis Equation

The reactivity ratio r₁ = k₁₁/k₁₂ and r₂ = k₂₂/k₂₁ (where k_ij is the rate constant for monomer i adding to chain ending in j) determine copolymer composition via the Mayo–Lewis equation:

d[M₁]/d[M₂] = [M₁](r₁[M₁] + [M₂]) / ([M₂]([M₁] + r₂[M₂]))

## Copolymer Types

- **Alternating:** regular A-B-A-B pattern; occurs when r₁ and r₂ are close to zero.
- **Statistical/Random:** monomer sequence follows statistical distribution determined by reaction kinetics.
- **Periodic:** repeating pattern like (A-B-A-B-B-A-A-A-A-B-B-B)ₙ.
- **Block:** distinct homopolymer blocks linked covalently; requires r₁ >> 1 and r₂ >> 1. Diblock, triblock, multiblock variants exist.

## Nomenclature

IUPAC convention: poly(A-co-B) where A, B are monomers and "-co-" indicates copolymer type. Branched copolymers add prefixes (e.g., branch-, graft-, star-).
